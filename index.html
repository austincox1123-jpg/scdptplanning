Definitions & Reference

Forecast Methods

Avg Weekly Run Rate (Fiscal Weeks)
Calculates average cases per fiscal week over the lookback window, then multiplies by the fiscal weeks in each future month. Uses the 4-4-5 calendar (editable in sidebar). Best when sales volume is proportional to the number of selling weeks.
Forecast(month) = (Total Cases / Total Fiscal Weeks in Lookback) × Fiscal Weeks in Target Month
Weighted Moving Average
Averages recent periods with configurable weights. Equal weights all months the same. Linear Decay gives more weight to recent months. The Window parameter controls lookback length.
Forecast = Σ(weight[i] × value[i]), weights normalized to sum to 1
Exponential Smoothing
Weights all history with exponentially decaying weights. Alpha controls reactivity (0.1 = smooth, 0.9 = reactive). Includes damped drift to capture trend direction without runaway extrapolation.
Level(t) = α × Actual(t) + (1-α) × Level(t-1); Forecast = Level + damped drift
POS-to-Shipment Lag
Analyzes the historical ratio between POS and shipment data offset by a lag period. Uses this ratio to project future shipments from recent POS trends. Requires POS data. The lag captures the delay between consumer purchase and replenishment orders (typically 1-3 months).
Ratio = avg(Shipment[t] / POS[t-lag]); Forecast(t) = POS[t-lag] × Ratio
Linear Trend (Slope)
Fits a straight line (y = a + bx) via least-squares regression over the lookback window, then extrapolates forward. Best when demand has a clear upward or downward trend. Values are floored at zero. Not ideal for cyclical data.
slope = (n×Σxy - Σx×Σy) / (n×Σx² - (Σx)²); Forecast(t) = intercept + slope × t

Data Sources

POS Cases
Point-of-sale data representing consumer purchases at the retail level.
Order Cases
Customer orders placed (shipment demand). This is the primary forecast target.
Invoice Cases
Invoiced/shipped cases. Reflects what was actually fulfilled, which may differ from orders due to supply constraints.
Blended
When selected as a data source, the engine blends POS and order data using a configurable weight to create a combined signal.

Forecast Backstop

A post-processing guardrail system that prevents any method from producing unrealistic forecasts. Enabled by default. Applied after the statistical method runs but before denormalization and MOQ rounding.

Layer 1: Growth Rate Cap
Limits month-over-month change to a configurable maximum percentage (default 25%). Anchored to the last actual data point. Prevents steep slopes from projecting unrealistic ramps. Also prevents sudden drops.
Layer 2: Historical Ceiling
Caps the forecast at a multiple of the historical maximum (default 2.0x). A product that has never shipped more than 500 cases/month won't suddenly forecast 2,000.
Layer 3: Trend Dampening
Decays the slope's deviation from the first forecast value using a damping factor (0.85 per period). Month 1 gets the full projected value; by month 6 the trend is roughly halved; by month 12 it's nearly flat. Prevents linear and exponential methods from diverging to infinity.
Layer 4: Outlier-Aware Blending
Detects if the most recent data point is a statistical outlier (>2 standard deviations from the trailing 6-month mean). When triggered, the forecast is blended with the trailing average so a single spike doesn't hijack the entire projection. Blend strength scales with outlier severity.

Key Concepts

POS-to-Order Ratio
When forecasting from POS data, the engine scales the POS-based forecast to order level using the historical POS-to-order ratio. The ratio uses a decay curve that gives more weight to recent months. Standard items use a faster decay; WT items use a slower decay to account for longer replenishment cycles.
Seasonal Indices
Computed from historical data as the ratio of each month's average to the overall average. When seasonality is detected, the forecast is multiplied by seasonal indices. Dampening is applied to prevent extreme seasonal swings when history is limited.
4-4-5 Calendar
A fiscal calendar where each quarter has months of 4, 4, and 5 weeks respectively. The engine normalizes actuals to a per-week basis and denormalizes forecasts back using the weeks-per-month schedule. This prevents months with 5 weeks from appearing to have higher demand.
Promo/Outlier Tags
Months can be tagged as Promo or Outlier. Outlier months are removed from training data so they do not distort the baseline forecast. Promo-tagged months have their lift computed from POS data and can be used to add expected promotional volume on top of the statistical forecast.
Demand Classification
The engine classifies each item's demand pattern:
Smooth — Low variability, few zeros. All methods available.
Erratic — High variability but consistent ordering. Smoothing methods preferred.
Intermittent — Many zero months but consistent size when ordered. Croston's SBA preferred.
Lumpy — High variability and many zeros. Most challenging; Croston's or simple average used.
The classification restricts which methods are eligible during best-method search.
Forecast Accuracy
WMAPE (Weighted Mean Absolute Percentage Error) measures forecast accuracy as total absolute error divided by total actual volume. The backtest uses a 2-month lag — the engine pretends it only has data up to 2 months before each test point, forecasts forward, and compares to actuals. Bias indicates systematic over-forecasting (positive) or under-forecasting (negative).

SKU Master & Assumptions
Upload a SKU list (only PRODUCTNUMBER is required). Each SKU carries editable assumptions: Case Pack (units per case), Stores (distribution doors), Velocity (units sold per store per week), Order Increment (case multiple each order rounds up to), Initial Shipment (launch sell-in cases), Launch Week, and an optional per-SKU Target WOS override.
Weekly POS
Consumer demand each week is Stores × Velocity (units), converted to cases by dividing by Case Pack. POS begins in the SKU's launch week.
Weekly POS Cases = (Stores × Velocity × Ramp) / Total Units per Case
Total Units per Case = Inner/Case × Units/Case (or just Units/Case when Inner = 0)
Velocity Ramp (by Formation & Ounces)
New items rarely hit full velocity on day one. With ramp enabled, each SKU starts at Start Velocity % of its full rate in its launch week and climbs linearly to 100% over its Ramp Wks. Ramp Wks is auto-derived from the SKU's Formation and Ounces — smaller and trial-type sizes (trial, sample, travel, single, snack, treat) gain traction faster (≈half the base), while bulk/club/family/value sizes ramp slower (≈1.5×); ounces scale it against a 16-oz reference. Any per-SKU Ramp Wks you type overrides the auto value.
RampWks ≈ BaseWeeks × clamp(Ounces/16, 0.4, 2.5) × FormationFactor
Velocity(wk) = FullVelocity × [ StartPct + (1−StartPct) × min(1, weeksSinceLaunch / (RampWks−1)) ]
Replenishment: Weeks-of-Supply + Lead Time
Each week the model orders enough to cover Target WOS + Lead Time weeks of demand, less what is already on hand or in transit. Orders arrive after the lead time. This builds a pipeline at launch and then tracks demand. Order quantities are rounded up to the case Order Increment (pack-size rounding).
Order = roundUp( (TargetWOS + LeadTime) × WeeklyDemand − OnHand − OnOrder , Increment )
Replenishment: POS-Fill
A simpler 1:1 mode — each week replaces exactly what sold through. Inventory stays lean; useful for steady-state sell-through rather than pipeline building. A Replenishment Start Date sets when 1:1 ordering begins: before that week the launch shipment alone carries demand (no replenishment orders), and from the trigger week onward each week's sell-through is reordered. Defaults to the launch date (replenish immediately).
Order Rounding & Minimum Presentation
Round Orders To controls the order multiple: Whole Cases rounds each replenishment up to a full case, or Full Layers rounds up to the SKU's Cases/Layer. Min Presentation (eaches) is a per-SKU shelf floor — the model keeps at least that many units' worth of cases in position, applied before the WOS target, so newly-launched SKUs hold appropriate on-shelf stock even when raw demand is low.
Sell-Through (ST%)
Sell-through measures how much of the on-shelf inventory sells each period: ST% = POS Cases ÷ On-Hand Available. A high ST% means you're selling through most of your stock — stockout risk. It appears per week in the weekly table and per month in the Sell-Through section. Max Weekly ST% is an active ordering guardrail: when set, the model looks ahead through the lead time and floors inventory at peak demand ÷ max ST%, pre-building stock so no week is forecast to sell more than that share of on-hand — a last-resort stop-gap for demand spikes (e.g. a promotion week pushing ST% toward 60%). It is applied after the WOS and minimum-presentation logic, only raising orders when needed. Max Monthly ST% flags (red ⚠) months whose aggregate ST% exceeds the ceiling. The weekly chart's month exclude chips drop the launch load-in so the chart autoscales to steady-state trends.
ST% = Weekly POS Cases / On-Hand Available · Max-ST floor: On-Hand ≥ Peak Demand / Max ST%
Export Assumptions (round-trip)
Export Assumptions (on the Per-SKU Assumptions card) downloads a two-sheet workbook: SKU Assumptions (every per-SKU input — velocity, ramp weeks, ounces, stores, case pack, initial shipment, launch week, WOS) and Scenario (all of Section 2 — launch/ship date, horizon, replenishment mode, lead time, target WOS, replenishment start date, and the ramp settings). Edit it offline, then drop it back on the SKU Master box to reload the entire scenario — per-SKU values and Section 2 settings — and keep iterating. This is the editing round-trip; it is separate from the forecast-engine export below.
Export Forecast & Apply to Engine
Weekly shipments and POS roll up to calendar months. Export Excel produces a re-uploadable monthly projection plus a weekly-detail sheet. Apply to Forecast Engine adds the customer to the main Forecast tab, with the monthly order projection saved as its forecast and the monthly POS projection stored as the customer POS projection — letting the new customer flow through the same grid, overrides, and exports as historical customers. Re-applying replaces the prior run.
